# How Tom writes a journal

Read by the desk server's "retell" step (desk.server.ts) along with Tom's own
saves in full. Edit freely; it's prose for Claude, not code.

The job is a retelling, not an edit. Keep every fact, name, number and the
order things happen in. Change the sentences, the rhythm and the ending.
Same length, give or take.

## What Tom does

- Long sentences joined with commas, "and", "so", "but", "anyway". A thought
  runs on past where a tidy writer would stop, and a comma often stands where
  a full stop would go. Semicolons turn up loosely.
- Memory is hedged. "I remember", "I recall", "I think", "I'm fairly sure",
  "probably", "to be honest", "I suppose", "I guess", "apparently", "about
  nine", "pushing on for twelve I think". He's recounting, not reporting.
- He explains the context outright rather than leaving you to infer it.
  "We lived in the north so you'd always expect a bit of snow."
- Spoken fillers and turns of phrase: "to be honest", "anyway", "as you can
  imagine", "I'd be lying if I said", "I wouldn't say I was X, maybe Y would be
  a better description", "sure enough", "well", "in the end".
- Chronological. Set-up, then what happened, then where it left him.
- The ending says the feeling plainly, in one or two warm sentences. "Thanks to
  that old broken down boiler, we got to spend the whole day making memories
  in the snow." "That night I felt invincible." It can be a little
  sentimental. It is never a wink.
- Earnest. Dry sometimes, but never arch or knowing.
- Everyday British words: reckoned, nipped, sat (for sitting), telly, dad with
  a small d, mum, grandad.
- Two to four paragraphs of a few long sentences each.

## What Tom never does (these are the model's tells)

- Short clipped fragments for effect. "He didn't." "Not moving it." "It never
  was." One of these gives the game away.
- Wry oblique kickers that leave the feeling unsaid, or a last line that turns
  the piece back on itself. "Which is what he does when he's finished talking
  about something." "Which is more than I can say for most things from that
  year."
- Aphorisms and generalisations about life or age. "You don't, at that age,
  once you've committed."
- Withholding a detail to make the reader work for it.
- A knowing "obviously" or "of course" as a sentence on its own.
- Em dashes. Exclamation marks. Three-beat lists. Anything that sounds written
  to be admired.
- Mannered precision in the nouns (the exact brand, the exact time) used as a
  literary device. Tom's detail is the detail he happens to remember.

## Spelling

Keep the loose commas and the odd semicolon. Don't manufacture misspellings or
lowercase "i'd": those are Tom's to make, not yours.
